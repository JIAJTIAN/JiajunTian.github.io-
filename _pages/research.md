My research asks how reactions and processing shape material structure, and how that structure determines function. I focus on soft materials and nanocomposites, particularly nanocellulose-based systems.

## Following structure during reactions and flow

**Question.** How do flow and mixing influence the structure a material develops?

I use in situ scattering and complementary measurements to connect particle organization with processing conditions. Our published work on cellulose nanocrystals examined how solvent and flow affect particle alignment and dynamics.

**Current work.** At ChemMatCARS, I am developing a continuous-flow microfluidic platform combining X-ray scattering, UV-Vis spectroscopy, optical imaging, and controlled flow. This builds on an in situ UV-Vis capability I commissioned. Mapping reaction progress along a channel will require a characterized flow field and mixing history.

[Related study: cellulose nanocrystals in process-relevant flow fields](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.langmuir.4c01846).

## Nanocellulose as a dynamic and functional matrix

**Question.** How do assembly and local interactions determine the behavior of a nanocellulose network?

My work uses SAXS, WAXS, and XPCS to investigate nanocellulose assembly, collective dynamics, and gel behavior. In our XPCS study, particle dynamics slowed markedly as cellulose nanocrystals self-assembled. Collaborative work on metal-ion-cross-linked hydrogels connects network structure with antifreezing behavior.

**Research direction.** I am interested in how nanocellulose matrices influence nanoparticle growth, organization, and accessibility. My longer-term aim is to connect these features to environmental applications, including contaminant removal and catalytic treatment.

[Related study: antifreezing nanocellulose hydrogels](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c01025).

## Resolving components with anomalous scattering

**Question.** How can we distinguish overlapping structural contributions in a multicomponent material?

Measurements near an element's absorption edge help separate its contribution to the scattering signal. At ChemMatCARS, I contribute to ASAXS/AWAXS commissioning through detector installation and alignment, geometry validation, and energy-dependent measurement workflows.

**Published work.** A collaborative study combined anomalous scattering and simulations to investigate diselenide-containing crosslinks in liquid-crystal elastomers. I contributed element-specific modeling to this work.

[Related study: diselenide-containing liquid-crystal elastomers](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.6c00059).

## From measurement to interpretation

Scattering offers an indirect view of matter. I combine complementary measurements and physical models to test which structural explanations the evidence supports.

That work begins at the beamline: I help users plan experiments and develop [scientific software]({{ '/software/' | relative_url }}) that keeps measurement conditions, processing choices, and uncertainty connected to the results.
